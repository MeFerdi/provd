---
title: Using Provd with your KYC provider
description: KYC tells you who your customer is. Provd tells you whether this release is safe, and proves it later. How the two fit together.
---

Provd doesn't replace your KYC. Your KYC provider (Smile ID, Sumsub, Youverify, Dojah, an in-house process) checks
**who your customer is** when they sign up and at periodic reviews: documents, liveness, and sanctions and PEP
screening of their name. Provd checks **each release**, at the moment the money is about to leave: the destination
wallet, who it has dealt with, whether it imitates an address the customer has paid, and whether the account looks
taken over. Then it keeps a signed record of each decision.

| When | Question | Who answers |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding and reviews | Who is this customer? | Your KYC provider |
| Every release | Is this payout, to this wallet, right now, safe? | Provd |
| Reporting | Can you prove you did both? | Provd's [evidence packs](/docs/evidence-packs), linked to your KYC files |

Provd never receives your customer's identity. It screens wallets, not names, and takes phone numbers only as hashes.

## 1. Link each check to the customer's KYC file

Send the customer's case or applicant id from your KYC system with each check. It's stored in the signed decision
record, appears on every hold and reject in your evidence packs, and fills the identity line of each draft
suspicious-transaction report ("from KYC case job-8812 (smile_id)"), so your compliance team opens the right file in
one step.

```json
{
  "chain": "tron",
  "intent_type": "payout",
  "counterparty": { "wallet_address": "T…" },
  "amounts": { "crypto": "250" },
  "customer": {
    "kyc_provider": "smile_id",
    "kyc_reference": "job-8812",
    "verification_level": "full"
  }
}
```

- `kyc_reference`: the id in your KYC system (letters, digits and `. _ : -`). Names, emails and phone numbers are
  refused.
- `kyc_provider` and `verification_level`: your own labels. They're recorded with the decision, not scored.

## 2. Pass on the signals your providers already have

Account-takeover risk is highest at withdrawal. If your KYC, device or telco provider tells you about recent changes,
send them as `account_signals` and Provd weighs them in the release decision:

| Field | Typical source |
|---|---|
| `account_created_at` | Your onboarding record |
| `sim_swap_at` | Your telco or SIM-swap API |
| `device_changed_at` | Your device-trust or KYC provider (re-verification on a new device) |
| `credentials_changed_at` | Your account system (password, PIN or email change) |
| `payout_destination_added_at` | Your account system (when this wallet was added) |
| `provider` | Who supplied the signals |

A SIM swap in the last 72 hours, or a new device together with a new payout destination, raises the score with the
reason spelled out. See [Scores, flags and errors](/docs/reference).

## 3. Report with both

When something goes wrong, your evidence pack gives your compliance officer the release side: the wallet, amount,
indicators, the action you took and the signed record. Your KYC file gives the customer side. Together they make a
complete suspicious-transaction report.

## What stays with your KYC provider

- Identity documents, liveness and biometrics.
- Sanctions and PEP screening of customers' names, and adverse media.
- Ongoing customer due diligence and risk ratings.
- Travel Rule data exchange (Notabene and similar), where it applies.

Provd covers what happens to the money: the destination, its counterparties, lookalikes, account-takeover signals at
release, your own fraud history, and the evidence afterwards.
